# Transport-peer enumeration is stateless and synthesized from live traffic; separate paths stay separate paths; matching device identities across paths is client-side logic, never core

Status: accepted (maintainer-ratified 2026-07-03, migration design grilling for the originating production firmware — an ESP32-C6 smart-agriculture node). Extends [ADR-0040](0040-net-plane-is-explicit-source-routed-only.md) (explicit-source-routed only) to the discovery/enumeration question; builds on [ADR-0027](0027-transport-and-connections-are-vertices.md) (path-as-route) and [ADR-0030](0030-can-transport-dynamic-in-transport-map-advertise-reassembly.md) (in-transport advertise map). Companion to [ADR-0045](0045-in-graph-authentication-per-hop-ed25519-tofu-noise.md) and [ADR-0046](0046-bulk-transfer-is-ordinary-auth-gated-writes.md) — the three ADRs of the origin-firmware network-rulings set. **Amended 2026-08-13: §Decision 1's "no vertices for peers" is NARROWED to *announce-census* peers — an ACCEPTED ws/tcp session on a `slot_server_t` listener MAY be an ordinary vertex (see [§Amendment](#amendment-2026-08-13-decision-1-is-scoped-to-announce-census-peers-1223)).**

## Context

The origin firmware is the first product adopting libtracer end-to-end: ESP32-C6 smart-agriculture controllers, a Docker gateway, and an Angular SPA, all as libtracer nodes. Topology: browser ↔ WebSocket ↔ boards, boards ↔ CAN ↔ each other, and the single-IP-entry case — one board reachable over IP with its CAN peers *behind* it — must work.

That topology forces two questions [ADR-0040](0040-net-plane-is-explicit-source-routed-only.md) left implicit:

1. **How does a caller learn what peers sit behind a transport vertex?** A board's CAN connection at `/dev/can` has peers announcing themselves on the bus (the [ADR-0030](0030-can-transport-dynamic-in-transport-map-advertise-reassembly.md) advertise mechanism). Does the transport vertex materialize a child vertex per announced peer — mutating the node's graph as peers come and go?
2. **What happens when the same physical device is reachable two ways?** With the browser dialing both boards directly, board B is `ws→boardB`; through the single IP entry it is also `ws→boardA/can→boardB`. Under path-as-route these are two different addresses. Is anything anywhere obliged to know they are "the same device"?

Both questions have a tempting answer — peer state on the transport vertex, identity matching in the protocol — and both tempting answers reintroduce exactly what ADR-0040 retired: per-peer mutable state on constrained devices and a protocol plane that reasons about multipath identity.

## Decision

**1. A transport vertex holds NO peer state and creates NO vertices for peers.** A read of a transport vertex's `:children[]` is **synthesized on the fly** from the live announce/heartbeat traffic of that transport kind (the CAN advertise map, WS link liveness); it is a snapshot of who is currently audible, not stored graph structure. An explicit-source-routed `FWD` through the transport vertex to an announced peer is forwarded transparently, exactly as before. **CAN peers never mutate any node's graph.** This keeps ADR-0040's invariant intact at the enumeration layer: every device stays O(its own links), never O(the network).

> **Amendment (2026-08-13), [#1223](https://github.com/avatarsd-llc/libtracer/issues/1223):** read "peers" here as **announce-census peers** — the CAN/UDP bus population this decision was written against, whose membership is published by other nodes. An **accepted** ws/tcp session on a `slot_server_t` listener is outside this sentence's scope and MAY be an ordinary retired-and-revived vertex; the synthesized-`:children[]` and no-stored-census rulings are unchanged for both. See [§Amendment](#amendment-2026-08-13-decision-1-is-scoped-to-announce-census-peers-1223) for the reasoning and the measurements.

**2. Separate paths are separate paths — by design.** The same physical device reachable as `ws→boardB` and as `ws→boardA/can→boardB` is **two unrelated paths**, two addresses, two subscriptions, two local vertices — the [ADR-0040](0040-net-plane-is-explicit-source-routed-only.md) redundant-links argument applied to topology walking. **libtracer never matches device identities across paths**, at any layer, on any node.

**3. The deduplicated "real graph" is top-level client/app logic.** The picture in which `ws→boardB` and `ws→boardA/can→boardB` collapse into one device node lives in **no device's memory** — it is a projection the *application* computes from walking its entry points, matching whatever identity its deployment trusts (announced device ids, auth pubkeys per [ADR-0045](0045-in-graph-authentication-per-hop-ed25519-tofu-noise.md)). It MAY ship as a side utility — e.g. a TypeScript package alongside the client SDK ([ADR-0034](0034-typescript-client-sdk.md)) — but never in core and never on devices.

**4. Two discovery layers, cleanly split.** Static/mDNS-style discovery (`discovery_static` / `discovery_mdns` in the module catalog) is **only** IP-peer bootstrap — "what can I dial" — and remains deferred. Vertex/topology discovery is **walking `:children[]` in-graph**, which already works today and which point 1 extends to transport-peer enumeration. Neither layer leaks into the other: dialing produces connection vertices; walking reads them.

## Considered options

- **Auto-mount announced peers as real child vertices** (the transport vertex creates/destroys a vertex per CAN peer). Rejected: per-peer mutable state on an MCU-class node, graph churn driven by another node's liveness, and cross-node coupling — one board's reboot mutates every listener's tree. It is the ROUTER-flood state model in vertex clothing; ADR-0040 retired it deliberately.
  > **Amendment (2026-08-13), [#1223](https://github.com/avatarsd-llc/libtracer/issues/1223):** this rejection stands **as written** — for *announced* peers. Every clause of it is an announce-census argument (cardinality over an id space, churn driven by *another node's* liveness, one board's reboot mutating every listener's tree), and none of the three transfers to a session this node's own accept policy admitted. See [§Amendment](#amendment-2026-08-13-decision-1-is-scoped-to-announce-census-peers-1223).
- **A discovery module in core that builds the network map.** Rejected/deferred: the only discovery problem the *library* has is IP bootstrap ("what can I dial"), which `discovery_static`/`discovery_mdns` cover when needed; a network *map* is a per-application projection with per-application identity rules, and putting it in core would force one identity model on all deployments.
- **Protocol-level multipath/identity** (the wire layer recognizes that two routes reach one device and dedups/prefers/fails-over). Rejected: this is exactly the auto-multipath machinery [ADR-0040](0040-net-plane-is-explicit-source-routed-only.md) removed, with the same failure mode — it destroys the failover signal that explicit redundant routes exist to provide.

## Implementation status (Brick C, landed 2026-07-03)

Point 1 shipped for the motivating CAN case; these are the concrete design calls, all reference-impl choices under this ADR's constraints:

- **Passive last-heard table, not probe-on-read.** The maintainer's "statelessly discover" resolved to a last-heard table inside `transport_can`, refreshed by **every** valid same-version frame another node emits and seeded by a join-time **hello** advertise (`slice_count == 0`, an ADR-0030 advertise-family form); entries **expire by silence** (`peer_ttl`, default 3 s). The table is **insert-only, one entry per DISTINCT node id ever heard** — exactly the policy the ADR-0030 identity↔path map already has — so growth tracks the bus *population* (structurally bounded by the 13-bit node-id space), never the request or frame rate; no artificial capacity is hard-wired (maintainer steer during Brick C: the reference binding runs on hosts — memory policy is the host's, not a baked-in embedded limit). A read of the listing synthesizes names from this table and stores nothing. *Probe-on-demand* (a `:children[]` read emits a discovery probe and synthesizes the reply from a bounded answer window) was evaluated and deferred: `op_resolver_t::resolve` is synchronous — a probe window would block a transport receive thread — so it needs deferred/await-style reply completion at the terminus first; it remains the recorded follow-on for buses where idle peers must stay enumerable without re-announcing (today an idle-but-alive node ages out after `peer_ttl`; periodic re-hello is the cheap interim if needed).
- **Wire visibility: advertise format `0x02`, module-internal.** The ADR-0030 advertise header widened 16 → 18 bytes with an explicit `target_node` (u16; `0xFFFF` = undirected) and gained the hello form. No graph-level op and no L2 TLV change was needed; the layout is documented in `can.hpp` and `docs/reference/14` exactly like the existing advertise fields.
- **Peer naming: `n<node-id>`** (decimal, no leading zeros) — the stable identity ADR-0030's structured 29-bit ID already carries; deterministic and collision-safe within the bus. The name↔CAN-id mapping is a pure codec (parse/format), not a table.
- **Enumeration surface.** `handlers_t::on_children` (L4, kind-neutral) lets any vertex serve a **synthesized** `:children[]` read; `transport_vertex_t` wires it for any link whose `transport_t::bus()` is non-null, emitting `POINT{ POINT{NAME <peer>}… }` from `bus_link_t::enumerate_peers`. The generic `:children[]` **member read** (direct child vertices as `POINT{NAME}` members) landed alongside — §4's "walking `:children[]` in-graph already works today" described the write/`create` side only until this change; the read side is now real.
- **Forwarding: integrated at the existing NAME→link seam, symmetric both ways.** `child_registry_t::by_name` falls back to asking each bus child's `bus_link_t::peer_link(name)`, so an announced peer's name is a routable next-hop segment resolved to a **directed** per-peer endpoint (only the addressed peer reassembles/delivers the group; bystanders consume and drop). Inbound, a bus link delivers frames tagged with the **sender's** peer name, which the router uses as the hop's inbound NAME — so the grown `src`/reply link names the bus peer and the reply is itself a directed send. No per-request state anywhere beyond the frame-carried routes (RFC-0004). Note the route segment is the peer NAME at the node scope (exactly how static connection NAMEs route today); [CONTEXT.md](../../CONTEXT.md)'s layered `/net/<conn>/<peer>/…` full-path walk remains the eventual addressing refinement of the same model.

## Consequences

- A browser node holding N WebSocket links has **N entry points to walk**; single-IP-entry with CAN peers behind it is the same walk, one level deeper (`ws→boardA/:children` reveals `can→boardB` synthesized from bus traffic).
- **Dedup, path preference, and multipath policy are client policy** — the SPA (or its side utility) decides that two paths reach one device and which to use; devices never do.
- The transport vertex's `:children[]` read joins the `:` control plane as another synthesized facet — no new wire behavior, no stored state, consistent with the stateless net plane of [ADR-0040](0040-net-plane-is-explicit-source-routed-only.md).
- The deferred IP-bootstrap discovery track stays deferred with a crisper boundary: when it lands, it answers "what can I dial" and nothing else.

## Amendment (2026-08-13): §Decision 1 is scoped to announce-census peers ([#1223](https://github.com/avatarsd-llc/libtracer/issues/1223))

Maintainer ruling on [#1223](https://github.com/avatarsd-llc/libtracer/issues/1223) (option (iv)), scoping — **not overturning** — §Decision 1. The original decision and its rejected alternative remain in force verbatim for the case they were argued about; what changes is the boundary of that case.

**The honest form of §Decision 1 is "no vertices for *announced* peers."** Its reasoning was written against an announce-census bus (the ADR-0030 CAN advertise map, §Context question 1 — "peers announcing themselves on the bus"), and it is sound there. It does not transfer to a session an accepted TCP/WS listener admitted, because every clause of it is an announce-census clause:

| §Decision 1 / rejected-alternative concern | Announced CAN peer | Accepted ws/tcp session |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Cardinality | insert-only over an 8191-wide node-id space (`kNodeMax`, `core/include/libtracer/can.hpp:65`) | bounded by the listener's `max_peers` — ~4 on IDF defaults (`kDefaultPeerCap`, `integrations/esp-idf/libtracer/httpd_ws_link.cpp:166`; the fake-IDF `max_open_sockets = 7` budget in `integrations/esp-idf/tests/host/fake_idf/esp_http_server.h:126`) |
| Bounded across churn? | no | **yes** — `p<slot>` is a pure function of the slot index (`core/src/posix_endpoint.cpp:409-428`, `core/include/libtracer/posix_endpoint.hpp:466-471`), so a reused slot revives the SAME vertex in the SAME slot; churn only bumps the saturating retire generation (`core/include/libtracer/vertex.hpp:69-96`, RFC-0024 §4.4 rule 3) and never grows the insert-only vertex map (`core/include/libtracer/graph.hpp:525`, `:1665`) |
| Driven by | **another node's liveness** | **our own accept policy** |
| Measured RAM | ~7.7 KB at 64 nodes — **~48 % of the ADR-0038 16 KB budget** | **~0.5 KB at 4 peers (~3 %)** |

RAM is measured, not estimated: `bench/bench_forward_heap.cpp` reports `vertex allocs=3 bytes=128` for a bare vertex — ~110–120 B per vertex on rv32 once the slot-deque entry is counted. At the CAN full node-id space (8191) the same accounting is ~960 KB, i.e. impossible; that is §Decision 1's argument quantified, and it is why the decision keeps its full force for buses.

**And CAN needs none of this anyway.** CAN peer names are identity-derived (`n<node-id>`), so "name, key and endpoint are one identity that no other peer can ever inherit" (`core/src/transport_can.cpp:199-215`) — CAN has no slot-recycling disclosure hazard for a peer vertex to close. The hazard is specific to POSITIONAL naming, which is exactly the accepted-session case.

**What prompted it.** [#1223](https://github.com/avatarsd-llc/libtracer/issues/1223) recorded a disclosure **confirmed by execution** against a real `tr::net::transport_tcp_server`: a session subscribes through a mount and closes; the producer keeps the edge; a brand-new session dials, lands in the same first-free slot, is named `p0` again, and receives the dead session's deliveries — byte-for-byte indistinguishable from a legitimate one. Slot reuse was deterministic (10/10 cycles, 3/3 runs), because allocation is strictly first-free. The peer-as-vertex shape is what makes the session's death a RETIRE and a stale route fail the RFC-0024 generation check.

**Rejected on the way here, recorded so it is not relitigated:**

- **An incarnation suffix on the peer name (`p0-7`).** Works today against an opaque return route and is the simplest thing that closes the disclosure. Rejected as **irreversibly wire-visible**: `enumerate_peers` output changes, every `src` grows, and it contradicts [ADR-0073](0073-naming-authority-the-application-mints-one-predicate-gates.md) §2's stated rationale for positional names. It also changes the premise of [RFC-0020](../spec/rfcs/0020-bus-name-not-a-routable-next-hop.md) §3's error-code reasoning ("a segment naming a departed session is indistinguishable from one that never named anything"), so it needs an amendment regardless. (The spelling `p0.7` seen in earlier discussion is *illegal* — `valid_segment`, `core/include/libtracer/path.hpp:59-61`, rejects `.`.)
- **A transport-owned session index + generation carried as a `PATH_REF` element.** Initially reported as amendment-free; that was wrong. RFC-0024 defines the element as *that host's own reference to its next-hop connection vertex*, validated by a bounds check into the pinned, insert-only **vertex map**. A transport-owned session table is a **different map**, and §7.1 Erratum 1 exists precisely to close the mis-route class where an element is dereferenced against the wrong map. So it needs an RFC-0024 semantic amendment plus a discriminator — it amends the document whose reasoning *does* fit its scope.

**Why scoping this ADR is the right instrument.** It amends the document whose reasoning does **not** fit the case at hand, and leaves RFC-0024's element definition — the thing an erratum was already written to protect — untouched. RFC-0024 §7.1's reverse-direction mint then works exactly as ratified. Nothing observable changes on the wire, which also makes it reversible in a way the incarnation suffix is not.

**Scope and what is unchanged.**

- §Decisions 2, 3 and 4 are untouched. So is §Decision 1's substance for buses, and so is the synthesized-`:children[]` model: a bus vertex still stores no census and still answers from live traffic.
- **CAN is explicitly out of scope.** It has no closure event by design (RFC-0009 §D.5 — connectionless kinds "have no closure event and honestly never fire it"), and `peer_ttl` is a *silence* signal, not a death signal; firing eviction on it would evict live-but-quiet subscribers. CAN's stale-edge problem needs its own answer.
- The realization is [#1223](https://github.com/avatarsd-llc/libtracer/issues/1223)'s five-step work order (peer vertices on `slot_server_t`; the RFC-0024 §7.1 reverse mint; producer-side validation; retired-peer-vertex eviction). This amendment is step 1 and changes no behaviour on its own.
